# Simulation results

Generated by `sim/experiments.py`. 3 engineers, 400-item backlog, 15-day sprints, averaged over 20 seeds per configuration.

**These are properties of a model, not evidence about your teams.** See `sim/README.md` for what the model does and does not represent.

## 3 · Expedites

Expedites pre-empt: they start immediately regardless of the WIP limit. Unplanned work queues instead. Both add the same amount of effort.

| Scenario | Days to clear 400 items | Cycle time p85 | p50–p95 spread | Mean WIP |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| No extra demand | 347 | 8.1 | 7.5 | 1.8 |

## 5 · Forecast calibration

Monte Carlo forecasts made every 4 weeks for the next 30 items, using only throughput the team could have observed at that moment. A well-calibrated forecast puts 50% of actuals below its 50th percentile and 15% beyond its 85th.

The backlog is enlarged to 1200 items so it never runs dry — otherwise the end of the backlog is mistaken for a capacity change.

## 6 · What an aging chart actually buys you

Flagging at the 70th percentile band. Note that asking whether the 70th band *predicts* passing the 85th is tautological — any item past the 70th was by definition open when it crossed it. So this measures attention allocation instead.

| Scenario | Items flagged | Share of total delay in flagged items | Days still to run once flagged |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| WIP 6, clean slicing | 25% | 83% | 3.9 |
| WIP 12, clean slicing | 28% | 88% | 10.0 |
| WIP 6, 60% paired items | 26% | 85% | 3.8 |
